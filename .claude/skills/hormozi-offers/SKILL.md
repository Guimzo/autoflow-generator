# Offers, Pricing & Business Models (Hormozi)

Tactical playbook for designing offers people feel stupid saying no to, pricing for premium, and picking the right business model for the stage you're in. Synthesized from `10x_pricing.txt` (10x Your Profit. 10x Your Pricing. 10x Your Business.) and `simple_business_model.txt` (The Business Model That's So Simple, Anyone Can Try It), cross-referenced with Hormozi's `$100M Offers` and `$100M Leads` frameworks referenced in those transcripts.

## When to use

Use this skill when any of the following is true:
- Building or rebuilding an offer from scratch
- Conversion rates are low (cold traffic not buying, sales calls not closing)
- Price is being set by competition, not by value
- Margins are thin and you want to 10x price without losing volume
- Choosing between selling services, products, info, community, or recurring
- An existing offer works but doesn't scale
- You need to write a guarantee, bonus stack, or offer name
- Competing in a commoditized market where everyone looks the same

Do NOT use this for: traffic/lead gen strategy (use a leads skill), sales script writing (use sales skill), brand/positioning work beyond the offer itself.

## The Grand Slam Offer Framework

Hormozi's core offer equation. Every offer has a **Value** that the prospect computes in their head. You want to make that number as large as possible relative to the price.

```
             Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value  =   ---------------------------------------------------------
                  Time Delay  ×  Effort & Sacrifice
```

Maximize the top, minimize the bottom. An offer wins when the perceived value so dwarfs the price that saying no feels irrational. In the simple_business_model transcript, Hormozi demonstrates this with the trash business: "if we ever miss a pickup day we give you 3 free months" — massive dream outcome (reliability), high likelihood (guaranteed), zero time delay (start today), zero effort (we do it all).

### Lever 1: Dream Outcome

The result the customer actually wants — not what you sell. Nobody wants a gym membership; they want to be hot at their reunion. Nobody wants accounting; they want a sellable business and more take-home cash.

How to increase it:
- **Specificity:** "Lose weight" → "Lose 20 lb in 12 weeks without giving up bread"
- **Status:** tie the outcome to identity ("become the kind of person who…")
- **Money/time saved:** quantify in dollars or hours
- **Pain removed:** name the exact pain the outcome eliminates (smell, embarrassment, missed practices)
- **Stack outcomes:** the offer delivers the primary outcome PLUS adjacent wins

Test: if a competitor could literally copy your dream outcome statement, it's too generic.

### Lever 2: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement

Does the prospect believe THIS will work for THEM? Three failure modes: "doesn't work," "doesn't work for me," "I can't make it work."

How to increase it:
- **Social proof** matched to their avatar (case studies of people like them)
- **Mechanism** — a named, proprietary "how" ("the Core Four method")
- **Specificity of process** — exact steps defeat vague promises
- **Guarantees** that carry the risk (see Guarantees Library)
- **Credentials & track record** — "40 months, 0 to 8M subs, $250M/year"
- **Demonstration** — do it once in public (free content is a micro-promise kept)
- **Third-party validation** — reviews, press, recognizable logos

Hormozi's branding point applies here: what *other people* say about your outcome matters far more than what you say. Engineer testimonials deliberately.

### Lever 3: Time Delay (MINIMIZE)

How long between purchase and first tangible result. The longer the delay, the lower the value. Cut it aggressively.

How to decrease:
- **Quick win in 24–72 hours** baked into onboarding
- **Done-for-you first step** so they don't have to learn before winning
- **Same-day start** — "we start taking your trash tomorrow"
- **Milestone outcomes** — give them small wins weekly so the macro outcome feels closer
- **Remove approval/setup cycles** that delay value

### Lever 4: Effort & Sacrifice (MINIMIZE)

What the prospect has to do, give up, think about, or risk. Every unit of effort kills value.

How to decrease:
- **Done-for-you > done-with-you > done-by-you** — always charge more for less customer effort
- **Remove decisions** — one plan, not seventeen tiers
- **Remove prerequisites** — no "before we can start you need to…"
- **Remove emotional sacrifice** — no shame, no public admission of failure
- **Handle the gross stuff** — "I love trash, I'll touch it so you don't have to"

### Using the equation diagnostically

If an offer isn't converting, audit each lever:
1. Is the dream outcome specific and desirable? (If no → rewrite)
2. Do they believe it will work for THEM? (If no → add proof/guarantee/mechanism)
3. How long until they feel a win? (If long → add quick-win + milestone structure)
4. What do they have to do? (If a lot → move DFY, remove friction)

## Pricing Principles

### Value-based vs. cost-plus

Cost-plus pricing (cost + margin) is the beginner trap. Value-based pricing anchors to the dream outcome's dollar value to the customer. If your accounting service helps a Med Spa owner make an extra $200k/yr in profit and sell the business for $500k more, $50k/yr is cheap — regardless of your labor cost.

Rule: **price is a function of value delivered to the buyer, not effort spent by the seller.** If you can't articulate the dollar value of your outcome, you can't charge premium.

### Pricing anchoring

Prospects don't know what "fair" costs — they compare to whatever anchor is closest. Control the anchor:
- **Compare up, not sideways.** Anchor against the cost of NOT solving the problem (lost revenue, consultants, lawsuits, time), not against cheaper competitors.
- **Lead with the highest-priced option.** Three tiers; the top one makes the middle feel reasonable.
- **State the retail value of bonuses** before the actual price. "$8,400 of value → yours for $997."
- **Use specific numbers** ($2,847, not $3,000) — feels calculated, not arbitrary.

### Premium positioning

From 10x_pricing: "you can get people to pay you 10 times more than they pay somebody else." The mechanism is brand — strong positive associations with your ideal who. Premium is earned through:
1. Exclusivity of audience (you serve Med Spa owners, not "everyone")
2. Keeping micro-promises via content before the macro-promise (build trust before the ask)
3. Delivering the product so that others speak on your behalf
4. Charging the premium price (price itself signals quality — dropping price kills the signal)

**The virtuous cycle:** make promise → keep promise → others tell others → new prospects arrive pre-sold → they buy at premium → repeat. This is what compounds into 10x differential over competitors.

### Why raising prices works

- **Better customers.** Higher price filters out price-shoppers and attracts buyers who value the outcome (and complain less, refund less, refer more).
- **Higher perceived value.** Prospects equate price with quality absent other signals.
- **Margin to deliver exceptionally.** You can afford the bonuses, guarantees, and DFY experience that raise Likelihood and lower Effort — which justifies the price. Virtuous loop.
- **Sales incentive alignment.** You can pay sales more, attract better reps.
- **Brand protection.** Low prices associate you with "cheap," which is nearly impossible to reverse (see Apple, Nike — they never race to the bottom).

Rule of thumb: **raise price until customers complain, then raise 10% more** — once. You will lose volume; you will gain margin and better-fit customers.

## Business Model Selection

Hormozi teaches that the model should match (a) what you already know, (b) who you can reach, (c) the stage of capital/audience you're at. Pick one and compound; don't diversify early.

| Model | Best when | Economics | Watch-out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Services (DFY)** | Zero audience, zero money, you have a skill. Fastest to revenue. | High margin per unit, low volume. Time-bound. | Caps at your hours unless productized/teamed. |
| **Productized services** | Services have clear repeatable SOP. | Can scale via team, better margin. | Requires documented delivery system. |
| **Physical products / e-com** | Capital to buy inventory, supply chain skill, or a hot trend. | Margin pinched by COGS + ads. | Cash-flow heavy, ads-dependent. |
| **Info products / courses** | You have a proven result AND an audience. | Near-zero marginal cost, high margin. | Requires audience/attention — don't start here without distribution. |
| **Communities / memberships** | You have recurring content + a tribe. | Recurring revenue, compounding LTV. | Churn kills it; must deliver ongoing value. |
| **Software / SaaS** | Technical skill + clear workflow pain. | Best LTV/CAC unit economics long-term. | Long ramp, capital-intensive early. |
| **Recurring services (subscriptions)** | Service solves a recurring problem (trash, cleaning, IT, bookkeeping). | Predictable revenue, higher LTV. | Must engineer retention. |
| **Licensing / franchising** | Proven unit + capital seeking owners. | Royalty/recurring. | Operational standardization required. |

### Decision rule (stage-based)

- **$0 and no audience** → Services for people you know (warm outreach), then strangers (cold outreach). The simple_business_model transcript's trash example is the canonical path: pick a service, get free case studies via warm list, convert to paid, then scale via cold outreach + ads.
- **Profitable services, no audience** → Productize + systematize + raise price. Start building content for distribution.
- **Audience growing** → Add info product/community to monetize the audience you already built.
- **Proven product + capital** → Consider SaaS, acquisition, licensing.

### The "one thing" rule

From the 10x_pricing transcript: "the central pillar of my brand is business." Pick the ONE core value proposition. You can sprinkle identity around it, but the spine is singular. Same with business models — run one until it's boring, THEN add.

## Offer Stack Design

An offer isn't a price tag — it's a stack. Each element multiplies perceived value or removes a reason to say no.

### 1. Core offer

The primary promise + delivery mechanism. State it in outcome language:
- Bad: "12-week coaching program"
- Good: "Add $100k in profit in 90 days using the Med Spa Profit System — done with you, weekly, until you hit it"

Pattern: [Specific Outcome] in [Time Frame] via [Proprietary Mechanism] with [Level of Done-For-You].

### 2. Bonuses

Stack 3–7 bonuses that each address a specific objection or adjacent pain. Rules:
- Each bonus has a name, a mechanism, and a dollar value stated
- Each bonus solves an objection ("but I don't have time…" → "Done-for-you setup bonus: $2,000 value")
- Total bonus value should exceed price by at least 5–10x
- Bonuses should feel like they could be sold standalone

### 3. Guarantee

Transfers risk from buyer to seller. Aggressive guarantees only work if your product works. See Guarantees Library below.

### 4. Scarcity

Real supply limit. Quantity-based ("only 10 seats this cohort"), capacity-based ("I take 5 clients at a time"), bonus-based ("first 20 get the 1:1 call"). Must be true — fake scarcity destroys trust permanently.

### 5. Urgency

Time-based reason to act now. Deadline on price, deadline on bonuses, deadline on enrollment window, price increase schedule. Always give a *reason* for the urgency ("next cohort starts Oct 1, after that waitlist").

### Full stack template

```
CORE: [outcome] in [time] via [mechanism], done [for-you / with-you]
  Price: $X (or $X/mo, or $X + % of results)

BONUSES (each with $ value):
  1. [Name] — [what it does] — [dollar value]
  2. [Name] — [what it does] — [dollar value]
  3. [Name] — [what it does] — [dollar value]
  4. [Name] — [what it does] — [dollar value]
  Total bonus value: $YY,YYY

GUARANTEE: [type + specific terms]

SCARCITY: [real supply limit]
URGENCY: [deadline + reason]

TOTAL VALUE: $ZZ,ZZZ → Your investment today: $X
```

## Guarantees Library

Guarantees raise Perceived Likelihood and remove buyer risk. The stronger the guarantee, the higher the conversion — AND the more selective you must be in who you let in.

### Unconditional

"Full refund, any reason, within X days." Highest trust, highest refund risk. Use when your product clearly delivers fast and refunders self-select out.

Template: "If for any reason you're not thrilled in the first [30/60/90] days, email us and we'll refund every cent. No questions, no forms, no hoops."

### Conditional (performance-based)

Refund tied to the buyer doing their part. Lower refund risk because non-doers are disqualified.

Template: "Complete [modules 1–4], submit [deliverables], attend [X calls]. If you do that and haven't hit [specific outcome], we refund 100% AND [additional bonus]."

### ROI / results-based

"If you don't make back [price × multiple], we refund the difference / pay you the difference."

Template: "If you don't generate at least $50k in new revenue in 90 days, we refund your $10k and pay you $2k for your time."

Use when outcome is measurable and you have strong track record.

### Better-than-money-back

Keep the money + give them more. Used when the dream outcome is hard to undo (time invested), or to signal extreme confidence.

Templates:
- "Don't hit the outcome in 90 days? Keep the program, we work with you free until you do."
- "Hate it in 30 days? We refund 100% AND you keep all the materials, tools, and templates forever."
- "If we miss even one pickup day, the next 3 months are free." (trash business example from simple_business_model transcript)

### Service-level / anti-specific-fear guarantees

Target the #1 fear the prospect has, name it, kill it.

Template: "Worried about [specific fear]? Here's our [specific fear] guarantee: [specific remedy]."

Examples: "Never miss a pickup." "Same-day response or we waive the month." "If the rep isn't a fit in 30 days, we swap for free."

### Stacking guarantees

Strong offers stack multiple: unconditional (risk reversal) + ROI (upside proof) + service-level (ongoing promise). Each targets a different objection.

### When NOT to offer a strong guarantee

- You haven't proven the outcome yourself yet
- Customer success requires heavy customer effort you can't control
- Refund fraud is economically viable in your market

## Naming Your Offer

A name makes the offer memorable, shareable, and pre-sold. Hormozi's approach (from $100M Offers, referenced in the transcripts): the MAGIC formula.

### MAGIC

- **M**agnetic reason why (new, announcing, finally, breakthrough, introducing)
- **A**vatar (who it's for)
- **G**oal (what outcome)
- **I**nterval (in what time frame)
- **C**ontainer (program/system/method/blueprint/challenge/accelerator)

Examples:
- "Introducing: the Med Spa $1M Profit Accelerator — for Med Spa owners to add $1M in profit in 12 months."
- "Announcing: the Trash-Free Home Method — for busy homeowners to never touch a trash bag again in 30 days."
- "Finally: the 10x Pricing Playbook — for service founders to double prices in 30 days without losing a client."

### Alternative formulas

- **Who + Outcome + Mechanism:** "For [avatar] who want [outcome] using [unique mechanism]"
- **Problem + Solution:** "The [problem] Cure for [avatar]"
- **Named System:** "The [Proprietary Name] System" — ownable, trademark-ready
- **Challenge format:** "[N]-Day [Outcome] Challenge"

### Naming rules

- Specific > clever. "Med Spa Profit Accelerator" beats "The Prosper Method."
- Include the avatar in the name when possible — self-selects prospects.
- One dream-outcome word (profit, weight, revenue, freedom, reliability) in the name.
- Test: say it out loud to a target customer. If they immediately ask "how?" the name is working.

## Pricing Plays

### How to raise prices

1. **Grandfather existing customers** at old rate (removes the churn argument).
2. **Announce the increase with a reason** — "we're investing in X, price goes to $Y on [date]."
3. **Create a buying window** — last chance at old price drives a revenue spike BEFORE the new price goes live.
4. **Raise incrementally if nervous** — 20% every 90 days until resistance appears.
5. **Change the offer when you change the price** — new bonuses/guarantee/name make the price change feel like a new product, not a hike.

### Testing prices

- **Split between prospects, not current customers.** Half see price A, half see price B. Measure close rate × price = revenue per prospect.
- **Start high, drop if needed.** Cannot go back up easily. Starting low is the expensive mistake.
- **Test anchors, not just prices.** Same $10k feels different next to a $30k tier than alone.
- **Watch refund rates at higher prices** — if refunds don't spike, you were underpriced.

### Structuring payments

| Structure | When to use | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Pay-in-full** | Always offered, discount vs. plan | Best cash flow, committed buyers |
| **Multi-pay (2–12 payments)** | Larger tickets | Opens access, higher total price (20–30% premium) |
| **Monthly subscription** | Recurring value, ongoing delivery | Best LTV, compounds |
| **Performance / % of results** | Proven outcome, measurable | Trust-heavy, huge ceiling |
| **Deposit + balance on milestone** | DFY projects | Cash upfront + commitment |
| **Revenue share / equity** | Long arcs, big upside | Aligns incentive, delays cash |

Rule: offer a pay-in-full discount of 10–15% vs. the plan total. Collects cash, commits buyers, filters tire-kickers.

### The "free" play (bfv — Big Fast Value)

From simple_business_model: give away a massive first experience to strangers as a trust-purchase. Works for cold outreach and early-stage businesses. Turn free deliveries into: (1) paying customers, (2) reviews, (3) referrals, (4) product feedback. Four ways to profit from one free delivery.

Rule: free is a *strategy*, not a default — use when you have no reputation and need proof fast. Not a long-term pricing model.

## Diagnostic Questions

When an offer isn't working, run this list IN ORDER. Fix the first yes.

1. **Wrong who?** Is the market growing, able to pay, easy to target, in pain? If any is no, the offer can't save you — change the avatar.
2. **Dream outcome unclear?** Can the prospect say it back to you? If not, rewrite in one sentence: "[outcome] in [time] without [common pain]."
3. **They don't believe it'll work for them?** Add: case study of identical avatar, named mechanism, stronger guarantee.
4. **Too slow?** Add a guaranteed quick win in 72 hours + milestones.
5. **Too much effort?** Move further DFY. Remove steps. Remove prerequisites.
6. **Weak/no guarantee?** Add conditional or better-than-money-back.
7. **Skinny bonus stack?** Build 3–5 bonuses each solving a named objection.
8. **Price alone on the page?** Add a total value calc; anchor to cost of non-action.
9. **No scarcity or urgency?** Add real constraint with real reason.
10. **Offer is fine — value comms broken?** The offer is good; the page/call/ad isn't communicating it. Rewrite hook + promise + proof + CTA (simple_business_model's ad breakdown: hook → retain → reward/CTA).
11. **Product fails to keep promises?** Fix delivery before scaling traffic. From 10x_pricing: the virtuous cycle breaks if the product under-delivers; advertising on a bad product accelerates death.

Always check in this order: who → outcome → belief → delivery friction → guarantee → stack → anchoring → urgency → communication → product.

## Decision Tree

### Stage 1 — Zero customers, zero audience, zero money

1. Pick a skill you have or can acquire fast.
2. Reach out to people you know (warm list) — offer 5–10 free case studies in exchange for reviews + referrals (simple_business_model pattern).
3. Deliver better than promised. Collect: review, referral, testimonial, feedback.
4. Convert free users to paid at a deliberate price ($29/mo in the trash example — cheap enough to close, expensive enough to test demand).
5. Once you have 10 paying customers, raise price 2x on new customers. Grandfather the original 10.

### Stage 2 — Some paying customers, no scale

1. Productize the service — write the SOP, name the method, fix the stack.
2. Build the full Grand Slam Offer (stack, guarantee, naming).
3. Layer in cold outreach (simple_business_model's third core-four channel) with BFV offer.
4. Start content (second core-four channel) targeting the same avatar. Every piece is a micro-promise kept.
5. Raise price to premium. Lose the cheap customers on purpose.

### Stage 3 — Consistent revenue, need leverage

1. Add recurring revenue layer (subscription, community, retainer).
2. Hire delivery so you move out of delivery into marketing/sales.
3. Run paid ads (fourth core-four channel). Ad = hook + retain + reward/CTA.
4. Test a higher-priced tier (DFY mastermind, 1:1) AND a lower-friction entry (self-serve).

### Stage 4 — Scale

1. Build an acquisition model — CAC < 1/3 LTV target.
2. Consider info product, licensing, or SaaS on top of proven delivery.
3. Protect the brand. Every new association trades some audience for another. Prune ruthlessly (the 10x_pricing "pull weeds" frame).

## Common Mistakes

1. **Cost-plus pricing.** Selling time × rate instead of outcome × value. Guarantees you stay commoditized.
2. **Selling the mechanism, not the outcome.** "12 modules" instead of "make your first $10k in 60 days."
3. **Weak or missing guarantee.** The buyer carries all the risk. Every dollar of risk you don't take, the prospect must.
4. **Too-generic avatar.** "Small business owners" is not a who. "Med Spa owners doing $500k–$2M" is.
5. **No named mechanism.** "Coaching" is commodity. "The Core Four Method" is proprietary.
6. **Bonus stack that's random.** Bonuses that don't kill objections are padding and look like padding.
7. **Giving free stuff forever.** BFV is a launch play, not a model.
8. **Competing on price.** Racing to the bottom brands you as the bottom. Hard to reverse.
9. **Price with no anchor.** "$2,000" alone is a number; "$2,000 vs. the $150k you lose by not fixing this" is an offer.
10. **Testing price on existing customers.** Use prospects. Grandfather existing buyers.
11. **Diversifying models too early.** Running services, info product, community, SaaS before any one is profitable.
12. **Breaking the virtuous cycle.** Over-promising → under-delivering → bad word of mouth. One cycle of this undoes years of brand.
13. **Making the offer about you.** Prospects care about themselves. Every line should speak to their outcome, their pain, their identity.
14. **Confusing branding with advertising.** Advertising = how you make it known. Branding = WHAT you make known. Good advertising of a bad offer accelerates failure.
15. **Hiding the price.** "Book a call for pricing" filters out the curious AND the qualified. If you must, give a range.

### Guarantee templates

**Unconditional:**
> "Try [offer] for [N] days. If you're not [specific feeling / specific result], email [address] and we'll refund 100%. No forms, no calls, no guilt."

**Conditional:**
> "Do [action 1], [action 2], [action 3] within [time]. If you do and haven't [outcome], we refund 100% AND give you [bonus]."

**ROI:**
> "If [offer] doesn't [generate / save / produce] at least [$X] within [time], we refund the difference. If you don't even break even, we refund 100% AND pay you $[X] for your time."

**Better-than-money-back:**
> "If you haven't [outcome] after [time], we keep working with you free of charge until you do — no extra cost, no new contract."

**Service-level (anti-specific-fear):**
> "We guarantee [specific service standard]. If we ever [violate it], [specific remedy — free month, 3x refund, credit]." (e.g., "If we miss a pickup day, the next 3 months are free.")
